## 2. Professional role definition

Describe the real-world role, mandate, scope of authority, accountability, operating environment and boundaries. Distinguish similar roles and avoid assuming that one title means the same thing in every country or organization.

## 3. Purpose and target outcomes

Describe what the learner should be able to understand, decide, perform, communicate and take responsibility for at the end of the program.

Separate:
- knowledge outcomes;
- analytical and decision-making capabilities;
- practical/technical skills;
- leadership and communication capabilities;
- legal, ethical and safety obligations.

## 4. Competency map

For each competency specify:
- ID and name;
- definition and scope;
- required knowledge;
- practical behavior or output;
- dependencies/prerequisites;
- assessment method;
- evidence required;
- proficiency expectation;
- applicable jurisdictions and contexts.

Do not claim professional certification or authorization unless independently established.

## 5. Curriculum architecture

Create a sequenced curriculum with stages, modules and lessons. For each lesson define:
- ID, title and learning objective;
- prerequisites;
- concepts and methods;
- sources and standards;
- practical case or exercise;
- assessment criteria;
- expected evidence;
- links to other competencies.

Explain why the sequence is appropriate. Include both foundational and advanced/integrative work.

## 6. Professional frameworks and jurisdictions

Identify the relevant:
- laws and regulations;
- regulators and supervisory bodies;
- technical and professional standards;
- licensing or certification requirements;
- industry codes and accepted practice.

For international professions, compare relevant jurisdictions explicitly. Distinguish binding law from voluntary standards and guidance. Record the date and applicability of changing sources.

## 7. Practice environment

Design realistic practice:
- worked examples;
- progressively harder cases;
- simulations and decision exercises;
- calculations and technical tasks;
- communication and reporting tasks;
- cross-functional and crisis scenarios;
- capstone/integrative project.

Protect confidential and personal data. Use fictionalized cases when real examples would expose sensitive information.

## 8. Assessment architecture

Define how knowledge and competence will be checked:
- recall and explanation;
- applied exercises;
- case analysis;
- practical demonstration;
- repeatability under changed conditions;
- independent or external validation, where available.

Set clear rubrics. Specify what is insufficient evidence and what would justify a competency claim.

## 9. Sources and research policy

List primary sources first, then authoritative secondary sources. Include source owner, URL or document identifier, relevant sections, date/version, jurisdiction and last verification date.

Do not invent references. Mark inaccessible sources and unresolved questions.

## 10. Learning records

Specify profession-specific evidence and artifacts, such as lesson records, casebook entries, calculations, reports, simulations and assessment results. Link them only to this repository's local Learning Log and Competency Register when the module is maintained here. Do not link, copy or import records from independent projects.

## 11. Maintenance

Define:
- which standards or sources change frequently;
- review cadence or trigger events;
- who can approve curriculum changes;
- how superseded lessons and assessments are handled;
- unresolved gaps and future directions.

## 12. Approval checklist

- [ ] Role and scope are clear.
- [ ] Competencies are mapped.
- [ ] Curriculum sequence is justified.
- [ ] Legal and professional sources are traceable.
- [ ] Jurisdictional differences are explicit.
- [ ] Practice and assessments test real capability.
- [ ] Evidence requirements prevent inflated competency claims.
- [ ] Links to Core and this repository's local records are correct.
- [ ] User has reviewed and approved the proposed module.
